## Aluminum extrusion standing desk

This year's splurge was a few hundred linear inches of aluminum extrusion. It supports a Ikea tabletop from the dumpster and is bolted on top of a pair of beefy electric standing desk legs:

![desk](/images/things2026/desk.jpeg)

(tabletop not pictured). This aluminum extrusion frame has proved especially useful for bike maintenance, hanging stuff, and arm-wrestling training.

## Neon picture frame

In addition to good coffee, Work in Progress cafe in Costa Mesa, CA sells prints. My best friend Cesar pointed out that he'd bought one of these prints, but was missing the big ~`25x19`" neon acrylic frame that accompanies it:

![reference frame](/images/things2026/reference.jpeg)

I know a little bit about fabricating with acrylic plastic, so we made him one:

<div style="display: flex; gap: 16px; justify-content: center; align-items: center; margin: 2rem 0;">
  <img src="/images/things2026/cad.png" alt="CAD" style="flex: 1; min-width: 0; width: 50%; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; object-fit: contain;" />
  <img src="/images/things2026/frame.jpeg" alt="real frame" style="flex: 1; min-width: 0; width: 50%; max-width: 100%; max-height: 450px; object-fit: contain;" />
</div>

The big challenge here was being limited to a ~`24x12`" nominal workspace on an Epilog laser, while the frame itself is `25x19`". Since the frame dimensions exceed the workspace, we had to design in some dovetails/joinery to assemble the backing and mat. The mat and poster snap to the frame with little neodymium magnets.
